# PyroWave on a slow Wi-Fi link
- **PyroWave's bitrate reacts to a link that cannot carry it.** The host already lowered the bitrate of H.264, HEVC and AV1 streams when the network lost more than error correction could repair; PyroWave streams were left out. A 200 Mbps PyroWave stream to a Retroid Pocket 6 on 5 GHz Wi-Fi (288 Mbit/s link) lost about a quarter of its packets and 706 frames in 55 s, and its bitrate never moved. Now the same controller cuts PyroWave's bitrate on lost pictures, then probes back up, and the stream card says "Bitrate limited by the network". Only a lost coarse block counts as a lost picture, so occasional detail loss changes nothing. The cuts stop at PyroWave's severe-loss floor (187 Mbps at 1080p60); below it the warning says to use HEVC or AV1 for that device. That leaves little room near the floor: the Retroid's 200 Mbps at 1080p60 can come down only to about 186 Mbps, and a stream at or under its floor (200 Mbps at 1080p120, floor 373 Mbps) keeps its bitrate and gets the HEVC/AV1 warning instead of a lower rate. PyroWave clients that send no FEC status get no reaction. The Retroid field case itself was not rerun. PyroWave streams above 500 Mbps keep their own ceiling, up to 2 Gbps. Measured on this PC through a loopback link limited to 250 Mbit/s, 400 Mbps PyroWave at 1080p60 for 60 s: 3.0.0-rc.5 lost 32 % of its packets and no picture arrived whole; now the bitrate settled between 250 and 290 Mbps, 2.7 % of packets were lost (most in the first seconds and in failed probes) and 81 % of pictures arrived whole. With 10 % loss that stopped after 30 s, the bitrate fell to the floor within 4 s and was back at 400 Mbps 16 s after the loss stopped. 0.05 % random loss left it at 400 Mbps.
