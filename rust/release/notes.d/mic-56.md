# Microphone level
- **The streamed microphone no longer clips into noise.** Windows' recording slider for Microphone (Steam Streaming Microphone) runs up to +30 dB, with 0 dB at 54: at 100, or wherever a voice app's automatic gain pushed it, the client's voice reached apps 30 dB too loud and turned into distorted noise. While a client's microphone plays, Rubylight now keeps that slider at 0 dB or lower, checking again every second on a thread of its own, apart from playback, so the level set in Nightjar is the level apps hear. A lower level stays as you set it. Measured through Steam Streaming Microphone on the RX 7900 XT PC with the slider at 100: 43% of speech samples over full scale before, none after, tones back at 0.0 dB from +30 dB.
- **A late microphone packet no longer breaks the voice off.** When packets arrive in bursts, as over a busy Wi-Fi link, the playout used to run dry and cut the voice into silence for 10 to 80 ms. It now fills the gap with the Opus decoder's concealment, fades it out if the gap lasts past 40 ms, and crossfades back into the voice when its packets arrive. The concealment never plays ahead of what silence would have, so the delay stays the same, and playback never waits for the network thread's decoder. Measured through Steam Streaming Microphone on the RX 7900 XT PC with packets held back 40 to 80 ms at random, nine runs each: silent holes in speech went from 73 (2.1 s) to 26 (0.18 s), median PESQ-WB from 2.60 to 2.84, and the time trimmed to keep latency down from 3.3 s to 3.1 s. The "microphone stopped" log line now also reports how much was concealed and any concealment that failed.
- The host log now names Steam Streaming Microphone's playback and recording formats each time a client's microphone starts, and Rubylight reads the playback format back after setting it, so a format Windows doesn't keep shows up as a warning instead of distorted sound.
