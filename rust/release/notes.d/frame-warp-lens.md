# Frame Warp lens
- **Frame Warp follows a turn all over the picture.** In a 3D game a camera turn moves the edges of the picture further than its middle. The host now learns the game's field of view from your turns and Nightjar turns the picture the way the camera does. Measured on the RX 7900 XT with a test game at 90 degrees: Frame Warp removes 96.0% of a turn's displacement at the centre (4.0.0: 84.2%), 91.8% over the whole picture (73.6%) and 90.8% at its edge (65.4%); at 70 degrees 93.0% at the edge, at 110 degrees 86.3%. From about 7 seconds of turning on, the learned field of view stayed within 3.5 degrees of the game's (86.6 to 88.9 for 90, 67.8 to 69.3 for 70, 107.6 for 110). Needs a Nightjar newer than 5.0.
- The motion measurement leaves out everything that stays put on the screen while the picture moves (minimaps, bars, a HUD's rim), not only the HUD the client holds still. Such pieces held the learned turn back near the picture's corners.
- Nightjar 5.0 keeps sliding the picture and now gets the turn of the picture's centre: 96.2% removed at the centre in the same test (4.0.0: 84.2%), 58.5% at the edge (65.4%).
- A turn left or right while the camera looks far up or down also rolls the picture, which Frame Warp does not follow: at about 7 degrees of pitch that is most of what is left at the picture's edge.
- Games that move alike everywhere (maps, side-scrollers) get no lens and behave as before: 96.4% removed in the same test.
