# Sending and encoding
- **The first packets of a large frame leave sooner.** An idle sender now gets each error-correction block of an H.264, HEVC or AV1 frame as soon as it is built instead of waiting for the whole frame, and PyroWave frames go out that way again. At 4K60 with large frames the first packet left about 0.55 ms sooner on HEVC and AV1, and HEVC's end-to-end time fell 0.5 ms; PyroWave's first packet left 0.13 ms sooner at 4K. Frames that fit one block don't change. `video_send_early_blocks` (default on) turns it off.
- **Less CPU for PyroWave at 4K.** Large AMF, NVENC and PyroWave frames reuse their buffers instead of allocating megabytes per frame. A PyroWave 4K60 stream at 600 Mbps used 3.4 % of a core less than 3.9.0, and packetizing took 0.09 ms instead of 0.32 ms.
- **Faster error correction on newer processors.** On CPUs with GFNI (AMD Zen 4 and newer, Intel Ice Lake and newer) the host computes error correction with it, with the same bytes: packetizing large frames was 2 to 4 % faster on a Ryzen 7 8845HS. `fec_gfni` (default on).
- **Fixed-rate pacing no longer claims a frame up to 1 ms late.** With `frame_pacing = grid` the host stopped waiting in an encoder output query close to its tick. HEVC 1440p120: end-to-end p99 16.47 to 15.71 ms, picture age p99 21.0 to 19.5 ms.
- **IPv4 clients with `address_family = both`** now count IPv4 headers in every send budget and batch size; some counted IPv6 headers before.
- **AMF output options, both off.** `amd_output_prediction` skips output queries the oldest frame can't answer yet, and `amd_output_thread` collects AMF output on a thread of its own. Under GPU load prediction gave no consistent gain; the thread cut picture age p99 by 2.5 to 9 ms but made the typical frame 0.8 to 1.7 ms later at 1440p120 and used more CPU.
- Experimental: **NVIDIA streams wake as NVENC finishes a frame.** On a D3D12 NVENC session the stream waits on the encoder's output fence instead of its 100 µs poll. `nvenc_output_event` (default on) goes back to the poll.
- Experimental: **`pacing_follow_client_link`** (default off) lifts the 800 Mbps ceiling of H.264, HEVC and AV1 on a wired route when the device reports its own link: the ceiling becomes 80 % of the slower of the two links.
