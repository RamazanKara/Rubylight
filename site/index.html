Rubylight {{download_label}} · Windows game-streaming host for Moonlight · formerly Butterpollo

Written in Rust.
Built for Radeon and NVIDIA.
Made for Moonlight.

Stream your games from a Radeon or NVIDIA PC to a laptop, TV, phone, handheld, browser or another PC. Rubylight drives AMF and NVENC natively, prepares every frame on D3D12 compute queues, and carries HEVC and AV1 in 10-bit HDR, with HDR10+, Dolby Vision and HLG for screens that show them, or full-colour 4:4:4 through PyroWave. It lowers the bitrate when your link cannot carry it, waits for your game's next frame under VRR, and times every frame from capture to screen.

A closer look

From the couch, not the desk

Ready to play in minutes

Open https://localhost:47990 on the PC and Home walks you through pairing and your first game: Nightjar scans a QR code, Moonlight enters a PIN. While you play, Home shows the stream's frame rate, bitrate and encoder timing, and what the host changed and why.

When something goes wrong, Help reads the host log and explains what it found, such as an AMD encoder hang, lost capture, a port in use or network loss, with the fix for each.

Every launcher, one library

Steam, Epic Games, Battle.net, EA app, Ubisoft Connect, GOG, Microsoft Store, Xbox and Playnite games join one list with their covers: one switch per launcher, nothing to type in.

Each game can carry its own stream, display and encoder settings, RTSS frame limits, a Lossless Scaling profile and a Radeon profile. They apply when the game starts and are reverted when the session ends.

Co-op on one PC

A game that Nucleus Co-op supports gets a “(Co-op)” entry. Each device that starts it joins as the next player, up to four, each in its own instance with its own picture, sound and virtual controller. A player who steps away keeps their slot for two minutes.

Or play in the background while someone else keeps using the PC. Co-op and background streaming

Your library on every screen

Nightjar, the client built alongside Rubylight, runs on Android phones and handhelds, Android TV, Fire TV, Windows, Linux and macOS. With Rubylight it adds QR pairing, every launcher's games with their covers, a session menu with Radeon controls, Dolby Vision, HDR10+ and HLG, clipboard, files, USB forwarding and co-op.

Moonlight and every other Moonlight-compatible client work too, unmodified.

What you get

The frame path, end to end

Native AMF and NVENC encoding

Each GPU's encoder driven directly, not through a generic wrapper, with AMF tuned per codec from VMAF measurements. Where the driver allows it, a lost frame is repaired with a small recovery frame instead of a keyframe.

GPU compute

Frame copies, colour conversion and scaling run on D3D12 compute queues beside the game's graphics work, so your game and your stream stop waiting on each other.

HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision and HLG

Ten-bit BT.2020 from capture to the encoder, with per-scene brightness measured on the GPU. Devices that ask get HDR10+, Dolby Vision or HLG on the same stream; others get HDR10.

Full colour, 4:4:4

A colour sample for every pixel keeps small text and red-on-black HUDs sharp: PyroWave HDR 4:4:4 on a fast local network, or 4:4:4 packed into HEVC and AV1 for Nightjar.

Slices and beam racing

H.264 and HEVC pictures of 4K size leave the host slice by slice, by default. PyroWave frames arrive in display order, so a client that asks can draw the top of the picture while the bottom is still on the wire.

Link-rate adaptation

When your link cannot carry the bitrate you asked for, the host steps down to what it delivers, probes back up, and tells the client what it did.

VRR-aware capture

With variable refresh, the host waits for the game's next frame when it is close, so the encoder gets a fresh picture.

Virtual displays

A display per device at its own resolution, refresh rate and HDR mode, shared or off, with exclusive, extended, primary and isolated layouts. Your own layout comes back after the stream.

Play in a browser

A WebRTC server streams H.264, HEVC and AV1 with HDR, surround and gamepads to browsers and Samsung TVs, paired with a PIN, with guest links for friends.

Controllers

A matching virtual pad for each player: DualSense with adaptive triggers, Switch Pro, Xbox or the Steam Controller with trackpads, back grips and motion, plus Xbox 360 (ViGEmBus) for tools such as reWASD.

Clipboard, files and USB

Clipboard in both directions, shared folders, file transfer and USB forwarding to the PC, each enabled per device.

Sound

Surround up to 7.1.4, uncompressed PCM or Dolby Digital for an AV receiver, night mode and headphone surround, game-only audio, and the client's microphone on the PC.

Radeon controls

Turn on Host control for a device and it can change Anti-Lag, Radeon Chill and more during a stream. Each change is undone when the session ends. Give a game its own Radeon profile and it is set when the game starts.

Stream presets

Lowest latency, Balanced or Reference quality, for the whole host, one device or one app.

A timing record for every frame

One CSV row per frame with capture, convert, encode, send and client times on one clock, so you can see where your own milliseconds go.

Rust throughout

Streaming, protocol, native helpers, the Windows service and the installer are Rust, with a web console for pairing, the library and live encoder timings.

Install. Pair. Play.

Three steps to your first stream

  1. Install

    Run the installer from the release. Moving from another host? Setup can bring its settings, paired devices, app library and covers along, or install beside it.

  2. Open the console

    Go to https://localhost:47990 on the host PC and create your local account.

  3. Pair and play

    Nightjar scans the QR code on Home; Moonlight shows a PIN you enter under Devices. Launch Desktop at 1080p60, then raise resolution, frame rate and HDR.

Why it exists

The card was rarely the problem

Radeon owners have long been told their cards are simply worse at streaming. Most of the time the card was never the problem: the path around the encoder was. Rubylight began as a host rebuilt in Rust around that path, from capture through conversion to the encoder, timed at every step, for everyone who wants to play away from the desk.

The same host now drives NVENC on NVIDIA GPUs, with the same capture, compute queues, displays, library and console around it. Whichever card is in your PC, the stream gets the same care.
