# Phase lock recovers
- **Streams no longer stay a few milliseconds behind after the PC was busy.** When the PC was busy for a while (a build, a game loading), the phase lock that times frames to arrive just before the client's screen refreshes could lose that refresh and never find it again: until the stream restarted, frames arrived anywhere in the refresh, about 7 ms later on average and with some judder, and the PC sent slightly more frames than the screen shows. Rubylight 3.1.0 already had this. The host now notices when it has lost the refresh and finds it again within a few seconds, also when the PC's clock and the client's run well apart: it measures how fast frames drift against the client's refresh and follows that.
  Tested: on a Retroid Pocket 6 (59.8 Hz) streaming a 120 fps window at 60 fps with Lowest latency, the previous host sent 60.4 to 61.1 fps and the fixed one 59.7 to 60.0 fps, locked to the screen.
- **Games running faster than the stream reach the screen in time.** A game at 120 fps streamed at 60 fps gives the host a new picture only every 8.3 ms, so the phase lock could never move frames earlier, only a whole picture later. It could end up with most frames just missing the screen's refresh, each waiting a whole extra refresh (about 14 ms) with some judder. The host now moves those frames only by whole pictures, a picture later when they would miss the refresh, so they reach the screen within a picture before it.
  Tested: in the same setup, frames from the PC to the Retroid's screen took 31.0 to 31.9 ms (median) and 36.2 to 36.7 ms (95th percentile) against 31.6 to 32.2 ms and 38.7 to 38.8 ms before, and 0.15 to 0.96% of frames were more than half a refresh off against 1.1 to 1.6% (two 35 s runs each).
