## desktop
- Windows: with Variable refresh rate On, a frame that follows another closely is held until the frame before it has reached the screen, and its drawing is finished while it waits. Before, such a frame could be presented while the first was still on its way, with the screen read as idle, and tear. Frames follow closely in a burst over Wi-Fi, and all the time when the stream runs at the screen's top refresh rate (120 fps on a 120 Hz screen). In our recordings of uneven content, frames that shared a screen refresh with the one before went from 39 of 1025 to 0 of 995 on a 240 Hz screen. A stream at exactly the screen's top refresh rate showed about 115 of 120 frames a second on a 120 Hz screen, each in a refresh of its own; before, it showed all 120 in one recording and had 231 of 1439 frames share a refresh in another. The log's VRR lines at the end of a stream say how often a frame was held and how long presents took to reach the screen.
