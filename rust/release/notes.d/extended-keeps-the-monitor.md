# Extended keeps your monitor as it is

- **An extended stream no longer changes your monitor after an exclusive one.** The end of an exclusive stream saved a Windows layout for that device with its virtual display as main and your monitor at the refresh Windows picks (60 Hz on a 120 Hz monitor). Every later extended stream from that device was handed it: the monitor ran slower for the whole stream, a game losing focus could put windows on the virtual display, and the layout could be learned as the device's own. Now the entry keeps your displays as you have them, and a stale one is put back when it shows up, also for a second stream's display, for a Remote Monitor's and when another stream's display leaves. A device that already learned the main-display layout this way keeps it until you change its setting.
- **A sleeping monitor no longer leaves a TV on alone when an exclusive stream ends.** A monitor that went to sleep during the stream can drop off the list for 3 to 6 s as it wakes, and with the virtual display already gone Windows switched on the only display left, such as a TV that was off. On a PC with such a display the virtual display now stays in the background until the monitor has stayed for 3 s or come back (up to about 13 s), then your layout is put back. Nothing waits for that: closing a game, ending a session or shutting down answer as before, and a new stream starts right away, ending the wait early. That stream takes the wait over: the TV goes off again, and on a layout that keeps your monitor the monitor is back at its mode once it returns. If the launch fails, your layout is still put back. Saving or restoring the display baseline and resetting display memory ask you to try again in those few seconds.
