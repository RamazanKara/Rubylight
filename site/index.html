Rubylight 3.0 · Windows game-streaming host for Moonlight · formerly Butterpollo

Written in Rust.
Built for Radeon.
Made for Moonlight.

Stream your games from a Radeon PC to a laptop, TV, phone, handheld or another PC. Rubylight drives AMD's encoder natively, prepares every frame on Radeon compute, and carries HEVC and AV1 in 10-bit HDR, or full-colour HDR 4:4:4 through PyroWave. It lowers the bitrate when your link cannot carry it, waits for your game's next frame under VRR, and times every frame from capture to screen. It works with Moonlight and every other Sunshine-compatible client, and with Nightjar it adds a console on your phone with your library, a session menu and Radeon controls.

All performance results and what each number means

What you get

The Radeon frame path, end to end

Native AMF encoding

AMD's encoder driven directly, not through a generic wrapper, and tuned per codec from VMAF measurements. A lost frame is repaired with a small recovery frame instead of a keyframe.

Radeon compute

Frame copies and colour conversion run on D3D12 compute queues beside the game's graphics work, so your game and your stream stop waiting on each other.

HEVC and AV1 HDR

Ten-bit BT.2020/PQ from capture to the encoder, with HDR metadata in the keyframes, checked by a test. H.264 is there too.

PyroWave HDR 4:4:4

A colour sample for every pixel keeps coloured text and edges crisp over a fast local network, with Nightjar or any other PyroWave-capable Moonlight client.

Beam racing and slices (experimental)

PyroWave frames arrive in display order so the top of the picture can be drawn while the bottom is still on the wire, and H.264 and HEVC frames can leave the host slice by slice.

Link-rate adaptation

When your link cannot carry the bitrate you asked for, the host steps down to what it delivers, probes back up, and tells the client what it did.

Stream presets

Lowest latency, Balanced or Reference quality, for the whole host, one device or one app.

VRR-aware capture

With variable refresh, the host waits for the game's next frame when it is close, so the encoder gets a fresh picture.

Radeon controls

Turn on Host control for a device and it can change Anti-Lag, Radeon Chill and more during a stream. Each change is undone when the session ends. Give a game its own Radeon profile and it is set when the game starts.

A console on your phone

With Nightjar, Home shows favourites, what is running and your library across PCs. A session menu closes the game or changes Radeon settings without leaving the stream.

Your library

Steam, Epic Games, Battle.net, EA app, Ubisoft Connect, GOG, Microsoft Store and Xbox PC games in one scan, plus Playnite sync, per-app settings, RTSS frame limits and Lossless Scaling.

Virtual displays

A display per device, shared or off, with exclusive, extended, primary and isolated layouts, HDR, and your own layout restored after the stream. Physical displays work too.

Controllers

Xbox, DualSense with adaptive triggers and Switch Pro pads from one signed driver. With usbip-win2 installed, a Steam Deck can show up on the host as a real Steam Deck controller.

A timing record for every frame

One CSV row per frame with capture, convert, encode, send and client times on one clock, so you can see where your own milliseconds go.

Rust throughout

Streaming, protocol, native helpers, the Windows service and the installer are Rust, with a web console for pairing, the library and live encoder timings.

Install. Pair Moonlight. Play.

Three steps to your first stream

  1. Install

    Run the installer from the release. Installing over Apollo, Vibepollo or Butterpollo keeps your settings, paired devices, app library and covers.

  2. Open the console

    Go to https://localhost:47990 on the host PC and create your local account.

  3. Pair and play

    Add your PC in Moonlight or any Sunshine-compatible client, enter its PIN under Devices, then launch Desktop. Start at 1080p60, then raise resolution, frame rate and HDR.

Why it exists

AMD rarely gets any love in this space

The protocol started as NVIDIA's GameStream, and the hosts that replaced it kept NVIDIA first. Upstream Sunshine has had a native NVENC encoder since 2023, while AMD still goes through FFmpeg's generic AMF wrapper. So Radeon owners have spent years hearing their cards are just worse at streaming. Most of the time the card was never the problem. Nobody had sat down with it.

Rubylight is a host rebuilt in Rust around the Radeon frame path, from capture to the encoder, for every Radeon owner who wants to play away from the desk.
